1. Isothermal expansion
	- The gas is in contact with the hot reservoir.
	- $T=const$. To keep the temperature constant, the gas must absorb heat from the hot source.
	- The gas expands and does work on the piston.

2. Adiabatic expansion
	- The system is insulated, no heat enters or leaves.
	- The gas still expands and does work.
	- Because it is doing work without receiving heat, its internal energy drops and the temperature falls.

3. Isothermal compression
	- The gas is now in contact with the cold reservoir.
	- $T=const$, but now the gas is being compressed.
	- Work is done on the gas, and heat is rejected to the cold sink to keep the temperature fixed.

4. Adiabatic compression
	- The gas is insulated again, no heat transfer occurs.
	- The gas is compressed further.
	- Work done on the gas raises its internal energy, so the temperature rises back to the hot-reservoir temperature and the cycle is ready to repeat.
