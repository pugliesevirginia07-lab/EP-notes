#### Intensive and extensive properties

An extensive property depends on the amount of matter.
- Mass $m$
- Volume $V$
- Internal energy $S$
- Number of particles $N$

An intensive property does not depend on the amount of matter, describing the condition of the material, not its size.
- Temperature $T$
- Pressure $P$
- Density $\rho$
- Concentration


#### Natural variables

The natural variables are the variables you choose to describe the state of a system.

Every thermodynamic potential has different natural variables, depending on which variable we conveniently decide to maintain constant (see better in [[Thermodynamics]] in section 6 *thermodynamic potentials*).


#### Conjugate pairs 

The most important conjugate pairs are always with an intensive and an extensive variable, such like:

| Intensive                | Extensive           | Expression                     |
| ------------------------ | ------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Temperature $T$          | Entropy $S$         | $dQ=TdS$                       |
| Pressure $P$             | Volume $V$          | $dW= -PdV$                     |
| Chemical potential $\mu$ | Particle number $N$ | The energy changes by $\mu dN$ |

These three pairs write the fundamental thermodynamic representation: $dU=TdS-PdV+\mu dN$.
